Most side effects of intermittent fasting are mild and fade within one to two weeks as your body and routine adapt. Many are not caused by fasting itself but by too little water, salt, sleep or food in the eating window. Here is what helps.

> If a symptom is severe or does not improve, stop fasting and check [the warning signs](who-should-not-fast.html).

## Hunger

**Why:** Your hunger hormone (ghrelin) is trained by habit and rises around your usual meal times.

**What helps:**
- Wait it out – hunger waves usually pass within 20–30 minutes.
- Drink water, sparkling water, black coffee or tea.
- Eat enough protein and fibre in your window; it keeps you full longer.
- Avoid very sugary last meals – they often lead to a crash and stronger hunger.
- Give it time: hunger usually drops noticeably after the first week.

## Headaches

**Why:** Usually dehydration, low sodium or caffeine withdrawal if you changed your coffee habit.

**What helps:**
- Drink more water and add a pinch of salt (see [water & electrolytes](water-and-electrolytes.html)).
- Keep your usual coffee timing during the transition.
- If headaches persist, shorten the fast.

## Tiredness and brain fog

**Why:** The switch from glucose to fat takes some getting used to; poor sleep and under-eating make it worse.

**What helps:**
- Start with shorter fasts and progress slowly.
- Don't drastically cut calories in your window on top of fasting.
- Schedule demanding work or training in your eating window at first.

## Poor sleep

**Why:** Going to bed hungry can make it harder to fall asleep; some people sleep worse with long fasts.

**What helps:**
- Make your last meal satisfying, with protein and some complex carbohydrates.
- Avoid caffeine after early afternoon.
- If sleep suffers, move your window later or shorten the fast.

## Constipation

**Why:** Less food and less fibre, plus too little water.

**What helps:** vegetables, fruit, whole grains, legumes and enough water in your eating window; movement helps too.

## Irritability ("hangry")

**Why:** Hunger and low blood sugar during adaptation.

**What helps:** tell people around you you're trying something new, plan fasts on calmer days, and eat balanced meals rather than snacks.

## Feeling cold

**Why:** Slight changes in circulation and energy use, more noticeable on longer fasts.

**What helps:** warm drinks like tea, a layer of clothing, light movement. If you feel very cold, dizzy or weak, end the fast.

## Dizziness when standing up

**Why:** Blood pressure can drop a little, especially when sodium and fluids are low.

**What helps:** stand up slowly, drink water with a pinch of salt. If it doesn't improve quickly, eat and stop the fast.
