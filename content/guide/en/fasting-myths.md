Fasting attracts strong opinions on both sides. Here are the beliefs we hear most often, with an honest verdict.

## 1. "Skipping breakfast ruins your metabolism"

**Mostly false.** Breakfast is not magic. In controlled trials, skipping breakfast does not slow metabolism, and what you eat over the day matters more than which meal you skip. That said, eating earlier in the day may have small metabolic advantages for some people, so a window like 9 am–5 pm can be a good choice if it suits your life.

## 2. "Fasting puts you into starvation mode"

**False for normal fasts.** Short fasts do not shut down your metabolism. In one study, resting energy expenditure actually *rose* during the first days of fasting, driven by an increase in noradrenaline. Long-term, heavy calorie restriction of any kind can lower energy expenditure somewhat – but that is about months of eating too little, not about a 16-hour fast.

## 3. "Fasting burns your muscles"

**Partly true – it depends.** Your body prefers fat and ketones during a fast, and growth hormone rises to help spare protein. Still, any weight loss includes some lean mass, and one 16:8 trial saw slightly more lean mass loss than regular eating. Eating enough protein and doing strength training largely prevents this.

## 4. "You can eat anything you want in your window"

**False.** Fasting does not cancel out calories. If you double your portions or fill the window with ultra-processed food, you will likely see few benefits. Fasting works best as a frame around normal, balanced meals.

## 5. "Longer fasts are always better"

**False.** The best-supported benefits come from regularly reaching the metabolic switch, which a 16–18 hour fast already approaches. Longer fasts add side effects and risks, and the extra benefits are mostly unproven in humans. See [what research really says](evidence.html).

## 6. "Autophagy starts at exactly 16 hours"

**Not proven.** Autophagy is real and fasting likely increases it, but the hour-by-hour numbers come mostly from mice. There is no simple way to measure it in people. See [autophagy](autophagy.html).

## 7. "Fasting is just an eating disorder with a nicer name"

**No – but it is not for everyone.** For most healthy adults, a planned eating window is a normal choice. For people with a history of eating disorders, rigid rules around food can be harmful. If fasting makes you anxious, obsessive or prone to binge eating, stop and talk to a professional. See [who should not fast](who-should-not-fast.html).

## 8. "Women shouldn't fast"

**Too simple.** Many women fast comfortably. Research specifically on women is more limited, and some report changes in their cycle or sleep with aggressive fasting. Start gently (12–14 hours), avoid long fasts, and don't fast when pregnant, breastfeeding or trying to conceive without medical advice.

> The honest summary: fasting is a useful, flexible tool with real but modest benefits. Be sceptical of anyone who promises more.
