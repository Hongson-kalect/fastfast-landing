How you end a fast matters more the longer the fast was. After a typical 16-hour fast, a normal meal is fine. After 36 hours or more, your digestive system needs a gentler restart.

## After 12–24 hours

You don't need special rules. Eat a normal, balanced meal:

- **Protein:** eggs, fish, chicken, tofu, beans, yogurt
- **Vegetables** and some fruit
- **Complex carbohydrates:** rice, potatoes, whole grains, bread
- **Some healthy fat:** olive oil, nuts, avocado

Two common mistakes: breaking the fast with a very large, sugary meal (which often leads to a crash and more hunger) and eating very fast. Sit down and take 15–20 minutes over your first meal.

## After 36–72 hours

### Step by step

1. **Start small.** A cup of broth or soup, a couple of eggs, some cooked vegetables or plain yogurt.
2. **Wait 30–60 minutes.** See how your stomach feels.
3. **Second small meal.** Add easy protein and cooked vegetables, a little rice or potato.
4. **Back to normal over the day.** Most people can eat regular portions by the evening or next day.

### Go easy at first on

- Large amounts of sugar, sweets, juice and white bread
- Deep-fried and very fatty food
- Large portions of raw vegetables or high-fibre foods (can cause bloating)
- Alcohol
- Very spicy food

### Common effects after long fasts

Bloating, a brief upset stomach or feeling unusually full on small amounts are common and usually settle within a day. Weight goes back up a little in the first days – mostly water and glycogen returning, not fat.

## Refeeding syndrome – the facts

Refeeding syndrome is a rare but serious condition in which levels of phosphate, potassium and magnesium in the blood drop sharply when someone starts eating again after a long period of very little food. It can affect the heart, muscles and breathing.

**Who is at risk:** mainly people who have eaten little or nothing for **more than five days**, or who are already malnourished – for example after serious illness, alcohol use disorder, eating disorders or very low body weight. It is managed in hospital with gradual feeding and electrolyte monitoring.

**For a healthy adult doing a 36–72 hour fast,** the risk is very low – but it is one more reason long fasts should be occasional, well planned and, ideally, discussed with a doctor. FastFast goals stop at 72 hours on purpose.

> Seek medical help if, after ending a long fast, you have swelling in the legs, a racing or irregular heartbeat, shortness of breath, confusion or severe weakness.
