Science has no single official definition of "breaking a fast". What matters is your goal. For most people doing intermittent fasting, the practical rule is simple:

> Anything with meaningful calories – especially sugar or protein – raises insulin, starts digestion and ends the fast.

Below is how common drinks and products fit that rule.

## ✓ Fine while fasting

| Item | Why it's fine |
|---|---|
| Water, sparkling water, mineral water | No calories; you need more water when fasting |
| Black coffee | About 2–5 kcal per cup; may even blunt hunger for some people |
| Plain tea (green, black, herbal) | Virtually no calories |
| Salt, sugar-free electrolytes | Help prevent headaches and dizziness on longer fasts |

Coffee on an empty stomach upsets some people's stomach or makes them jittery. If that happens, switch to tea or take it weaker.

## ~ Use sparingly

| Item | The catch |
|---|---|
| Zero-calorie sweeteners, diet soda | Almost no calories, but sweet taste can increase cravings in some people; evidence on insulin effects is mixed |
| Sugar-free gum and mints | A few calories each; fine in moderation |
| A splash of milk in coffee | Small amounts (a tablespoon) are a common compromise, but strictly it is food |
| Lemon in water | A slice adds negligible calories |

If something in this list makes the fast feel harder, drop it.

## ✗ Ends the fast

- Milk, cream and creamers in more than a splash, sugar, honey, syrups
- Juice, smoothies, sweetened drinks, "detox" teas with sugar
- Protein shakes, BCAAs, collagen, meal replacements
- "Bulletproof" coffee with butter or MCT oil (a lot of calories, even if insulin rises little)
- Bone broth (some people use it deliberately on long fasts, but it contains calories and protein)
- Alcohol

## Supplements and medicine

- **Prescribed medication:** always take it as prescribed. If it must be taken with food, ask your doctor about timing or choose an eating window that fits. Never skip medicine to keep a fast.
- **Multivitamins and fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K):** absorb better with food, so take them in your eating window.
- **Fish oil:** has a few calories; easier on the stomach with a meal.
- **Magnesium, potassium, sodium:** fine during the fast and helpful on longer ones (see [water & electrolytes](water-and-electrolytes.html)).
- **Gummy vitamins:** usually contain sugar – take them with meals.

## Does it matter if I "break" it a little?

Not much. A splash of milk on a 16-hour fast does not erase your progress, and it is better than quitting fasting altogether because you dislike black coffee. Perfection is less important than a routine you can keep. If you are doing a long fast for a specific reason, or following medical advice, be stricter.
