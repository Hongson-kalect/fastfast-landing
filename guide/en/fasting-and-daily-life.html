🍽️ Fasting in real life – work, sleep, social meals and travel

Practical tips to keep a fasting routine with family dinners, business lunches, night shifts, holidays and time zones – without being rigid.

2 min read · Updated 2026-10-29

  3. Work and focus

The hardest part of fasting is rarely the science – it is fitting it into a life with other people, work and plans. A routine that bends a little lasts much longer than one that breaks at the first dinner invitation.

Pick a window that fits your life

  • Family dinners at 7 pm? A window like 11 am–7 pm keeps you at the table.
  • Early riser and early bed? An earlier window (8 am–4 pm) may suit you and may be slightly better metabolically.
  • Late working hours? A later window is fine; consistency matters more than the exact hours.

Choose the window you can keep on most days, not the one that looks ideal on paper.

Social meals

  • Move your window, don't skip the event. If there is a dinner on Saturday, start your fast later that morning.
  • Say less. "I'm not hungry yet" is usually enough; you don't need to explain fasting.
  • Use flexible days. Many people fast 5–6 days a week and relax on weekends. That still counts.
  • Celebrations: enjoy them. One long dinner won't undo weeks of routine.

Work and focus

  • Most people find the late-morning part of a fast the most focused time once adapted.
  • Avoid scheduling your hardest work at the end of a long fast in the first weeks.
  • Keep water and black coffee or tea at your desk.

Sleep

  • Finish your last meal two to three hours before bed if you can – it often improves sleep.
  • Don't go to bed very hungry; make your last meal satisfying.
  • If longer fasts disturb your sleep, keep them short. Sleep affects hunger, mood and weight more than an extra hour of fasting.

Night shifts

Shift work makes timing tricky. Keep the idea simple: fast during your main sleep period plus a few hours, and eat during your waking hours. Avoid big meals in the middle of a night shift if you can; a smaller meal works better. If you have health conditions, talk to your doctor – shift work already stresses metabolism.

Travel and time zones

  • On long flights, it is often easier to fast until breakfast time at your destination.
  • Keep water close; cabins are dry.
  • Don't chase perfect numbers while travelling. Picking up again when you are home is what matters.

Holidays and festivals

Seasonal holidays come with big meals. Plan lighter fasts around them, enjoy the food, and restart afterwards. In December, FastFast's Christmas theme is free – a small reminder that fasting is supposed to fit your life, not replace it.

This article is general information, not medical advice. Talk to a doctor before fasting if you have a medical condition, are pregnant or take medication.
