🏃 Exercise and fasting – training without losing muscle or energy

Can you work out while fasting? How to time cardio and strength training, how much protein to aim for, and when to train in your eating window instead.

Exercise and intermittent fasting work well together – as long as you match the type of training to where you are in your fast and eat enough in your window.

Can I exercise while fasting?

Yes, for most healthy people. What works depends on intensity:

Type of exerciseDuring a 12–18 h fastNotes
Walking, easy cycling, yoga✓ GreatLow intensity runs well on fat
Moderate cardio (30–60 min)✓ Usually fineDrink water; slow down if you feel light-headed
Strength training~ PossibleMany people lift better after eating; eat protein soon after
HIIT, sprints, long endurance sessions~ HarderPerformance often drops without food; better in your eating window
Training during 24 h+ fasts✗ Keep it lightWalking and stretching only

Timing your eating window around training

The simplest approach: train toward the end of your fast, then break the fast with a protein-rich meal. For example, with a 12 pm–8 pm window, train at 11 am and eat at 12 pm.

If you train in the evening, place your window so that you eat both before and after – for example 1 pm–9 pm.

For hard or long sessions (intervals, races, heavy lifting), training fed usually gives better performance. Your goals matter more than staying in "fasted" mode.

Protect your muscle

Weight loss always includes some lean tissue, and one 16:8 trial found slightly more lean mass loss than regular eating. Two habits largely prevent this:

  1. Enough protein. Sports nutrition guidelines suggest about 1.4–2.0 g of protein per kg of body weight per day for active people. In a short window, spread it over two or three meals of 25–40 g each.
  2. Strength training two or three times a week.

Hydration and electrolytes

You lose water and sodium through sweat, on top of what fasting already removes. Drink before and after training, and on long or hot sessions add a pinch of salt or a sugar-free electrolyte drink. See water & electrolytes.

Warning signs during exercise

Stop, sit down and eat or drink something with sugar if you feel:

  • Dizziness, tunnel vision or nausea
  • Shaky, sweaty and weak (possible low blood sugar)
  • Heart palpitations or chest discomfort (seek medical help)

People with diabetes, heart conditions or on blood pressure medication should discuss fasted exercise with their doctor.
