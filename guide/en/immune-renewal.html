🛡️ Fasting and immune renewal – what the studies actually show

Where the idea that long fasts "reset" the immune system comes from, what was found in mice and in small human trials, and how to read it sensibly.

2 min read · Updated 2026-10-29

  1. Where the idea comes from
  2. How to read this
  3. What FastFast shows

You may have read that a 72-hour fast "regenerates your entire immune system". The claim comes from real, well-published research – but the details matter a lot.

Where the idea comes from

Most of it traces back to work by Valter Longo and colleagues at the University of Southern California.

The mouse studies

In a 2014 study in Cell Stem Cell, mice went through repeated cycles of 48–72 hours of fasting. During each fast, their white blood cell counts dropped. After they ate again, blood-forming stem cells became more active and rebuilt the immune cell population. Mice given chemotherapy were better protected from immune damage when they fasted around the treatment.

A key part of the mechanism was a fall in IGF-1, a growth hormone signal, and in a related pathway called PKA. Lower IGF-1 appeared to push stem cells into a self-renewal mode.

The human data

The same paper included a small pilot study in people receiving chemotherapy. Fasting around chemotherapy sessions was associated with less drop in some immune cells. This was an early, small trial, not proof of benefit.

The "fasting-mimicking diet"

In 2015, the same group published a study of a fasting-mimicking diet: five days a month of a specific low-calorie, low-protein plan. In mice it was linked to regeneration in several tissues and a longer healthspan. In a small human trial, three monthly cycles lowered IGF-1 and some risk markers such as fasting glucose.

How to read this

ClaimWhat the evidence says
Fasting renews immune cells in miceYes, in well-designed studies
It does the same in healthy humansNot shown yet
It protects immune cells during chemotherapyEarly, promising human data; only under oncologist supervision
"A 72-hour fast resets your immune system"An oversimplification of animal findings

Prolonged fasting carries real risks, especially for people who are ill, underweight or on medication. Anyone with cancer should only consider fasting with their oncology team.

What FastFast shows

The Immune renewal phase on the FastFast timer starts at about 36 hours, based on this research. We show it so you know what the research is exploring, not as a promise. A 16:8 routine you can keep is far more likely to help your health than occasional extreme fasts.
