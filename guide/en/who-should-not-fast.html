⚠️ Who should not fast – and the warning signs to stop

Groups who should avoid fasting or only fast with medical supervision, medication considerations, and the symptoms that mean you should stop and eat.

2 min read · Updated 2026-10-29

  1. Don't fast without medical supervision if you
  2. Before long fasts (over 24 hours)
  3. Stop and eat if you notice
  4. Ending early is not failure

For most healthy adults, a daily 12–16 hour fast is safe. But fasting is a real stress on the body, and for some people the risks outweigh any benefit. Please read this before you start – and especially before any fast longer than a day.

Don't fast without medical supervision if you

  • Are pregnant, breastfeeding or trying to conceive. You and your baby need steady nutrition.
  • Are under 18. Growing bodies need regular energy and nutrients.
  • Are underweight (BMI under 18.5) or have unintentionally lost weight.
  • Have or had an eating disorder – anorexia, bulimia, binge eating disorder or orthorexia. Rigid food rules can trigger relapse.
  • Have type 1 diabetes, or type 2 diabetes treated with insulin or sulfonylureas. These medicines can cause dangerously low blood sugar when you don't eat.
  • Take medication that must be taken with food or that affects blood pressure, fluids or blood sugar (for example diuretics, some heart and blood-pressure drugs, lithium).
  • Have kidney disease, heart disease, gout, low blood pressure or a history of fainting.
  • Are recovering from surgery, a serious illness or an infection.
  • Are an older adult with frailty or muscle loss.

If you are in one of these groups, fasting may still be possible – but only with your doctor's plan, often with adjusted medication.

Before long fasts (over 24 hours)

Talk to a doctor first, even if you are healthy. Prolonged fasts increase the risk of dizziness, low blood pressure, electrolyte problems and, rarely, refeeding problems when you start eating again. Plan for water & electrolytes and read breaking a long fast.

Stop and eat if you notice

SymptomWhy it matters
Dizziness, light-headedness, fainting or blurred visionLow blood pressure or low blood sugar
Shaking, cold sweats, confusion, slurred speechSigns of hypoglycaemia – eat something with sugar now
A racing, pounding or irregular heartbeatPossible electrolyte problem
Severe weakness or being unable to concentrate on simple tasksYour body is struggling
Vomiting, severe stomach pain or diarrhoeaRisk of dehydration
Intense anxiety, obsessive thoughts about food or binge urgesFasting may be harming your relationship with food

If symptoms are severe – chest pain, fainting, confusion or trouble breathing – seek emergency medical help.

Ending early is not failure

The point of fasting is your health, not the number on a timer. Ending a fast early because you feel unwell is the right decision. In FastFast, shields protect your streak on rest days, so one shorter or skipped day won't erase your progress – see streaks, Habit and shields.
