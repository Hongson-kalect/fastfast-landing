🗓️ Fasting schedules explained – 16:8, 18:6, OMAD, 5:2 and longer fasts

Compare the most common intermittent fasting schedules, who each one suits, and how to choose and progress safely.

3 min read · Updated 2026-10-29

  1. Daily time-restricted eating
  2. Weekly patterns
  3. Longer fasts (36–72 hours)
  4. How to choose
  5. Progressing safely

There is no single "best" fasting schedule. The best one is the one you can repeat week after week without feeling miserable. Here is how the common options compare.

Daily time-restricted eating

These schedules repeat every day. The first number is hours of fasting, the second is the eating window.

12:12 and 14:10 – the warm-up

Stop eating after dinner and have a slightly later breakfast. Many people already do 12:12 without noticing. It is a gentle way to build the habit.

16:8 – the classic

The most studied and most popular schedule. A typical day: eat between 12 pm and 8 pm, fast from 8 pm to noon. Most of the fast happens while you sleep. In FastFast this goal is called Intermittent.

18:6 – a step up

Two more hours of fasting, a 6-hour window (for example 1 pm–7 pm). Useful if 16:8 feels easy and you want a little more. In FastFast: Fat Burner.

20:4 – the "warrior" window

Two meals or one large meal plus a snack in 4 hours. It is easy to under-eat protein in such a short window, so plan your meals. In FastFast: Warrior.

OMAD (23:1) – one meal a day

One complete, nutritious meal per day. Simple, but demanding: it is hard to fit enough protein, fibre and micronutrients into one meal, and it does not suit everyone. In FastFast: OMAD.

Weekly patterns

5:2

Eat normally five days a week; on two non-consecutive days eat very little (around 500–600 kcal). Trials show weight loss similar to daily calorie restriction.

Alternate-day fasting

Alternate between normal days and fasting or very-low-calorie days. A one-year trial found results similar to ordinary calorie restriction, but more people dropped out.

Longer fasts (36–72 hours)

GoalWhat it isNotes
36 h (Monk)Skip one full day, e.g. dinner to breakfast two days laterPick a calm day without hard training
48 h (Extended)Two days without foodElectrolytes are essential
72 h (Prolonged)Three days without foodOnly for experienced, healthy adults, ideally with medical advice

Long fasts are occasional tools, not daily routines. Read breaking a long fast and water & electrolytes before trying one.

How to choose

  • New to fasting? Start at 12–14 hours and move to 16:8 over one to two weeks.
  • Busy mornings? 16:8 with a late breakfast or skipped breakfast fits naturally.
  • Social dinners? An earlier window (8 am–4 pm) may be healthier, but a later window you can keep is better than an ideal one you can't.
  • Dislike daily rules? Try 5:2.
  • Training hard? Keep the window around your training and eat enough protein – see exercise and fasting.

More hours is not automatically better. If a longer schedule makes you overeat, sleep badly or feel unwell, step back. Consistency beats intensity.

Progressing safely

  1. Stay at a level until it feels easy for at least a week.
  2. Increase by one or two hours at a time.
  3. Keep your eating window full of real food, not just whatever is left.
  4. Treat 36 h+ fasts as rare experiments, and stop if you notice any warning signs.
